## 🥁 Rhythm Profile Section — How It Works

### What it shows

The **Rhythm Profile** section answers: *"Of all the work sessions you've done, which focus levels (Agaman, Sparsha, Sthiti, etc.) did you use the most, by total minutes and share %?"*

Each row shows:
- **Name + session length** — the preset's name and its work block duration (e.g. Sthiti = 36 min)
- **Focus time** — total weighted focus minutes attributed to that rhythm
- **Bar + %** — its share of the total, shown as a progress bar relative to the top entry

---

### Step-by-step Pipeline

#### 1. Backend: Grouping sessions into "bouts" ([`db.py:564–595`](file:///Users/harsh24/Desktop/Projects/Muhurata-focus-timer/db.py#L564-L595))

Every **productive segment** (`kind = 'work'`, `'extend'`, or `'flow'`) is fetched from `activity_segments`, ordered chronologically. The algorithm groups consecutive work segments into **bouts** (continuous focus sessions):

```
RHYTHM_BOUT_GAP_SEC = 120  ← two consecutive segments within 2 minutes = same bout
RHYTHM_NOISE_FLOOR_SEC = 300  ← bouts shorter than 5 minutes are ignored (noise)
```

A bout is finalized when:
- A new productive segment starts **more than 2 minutes** after the previous one ended, OR
- The loop ends

#### 2. Backend: Assigning a bout to rhythm levels ([`db.py:459–484`](file:///Users/harsh24/Desktop/Projects/Muhurata-focus-timer/db.py#L459-L484))

`_rhythm_weights_for_bout(duration_sec, rhythms)` maps a bout's total duration to the 8 preset levels by **linear interpolation**:

| Bout duration | Attribution |
|---|---|
| ≤ 12 min (Agaman's work_sec) | 100% → Agaman |
| = exactly one level's work_sec | 100% → that level |
| between two levels (e.g. 30 min, between Sparsha 24 and Sthiti 36) | split proportionally: closer to Sparsha → more weight to Sparsha |
| ≥ 96 min (Ekagra's work_sec) | 100% → Ekagra |

#### 3. Backend: Building the final profile ([`db.py:427–441`](file:///Users/harsh24/Desktop/Projects/Muhurata-focus-timer/db.py#L427-L441))

`_rhythm_profile_from_weights(...)` converts accumulated seconds to:
- `focus_minutes` = `weighted_sec / 60`
- `focus_pct` = `weighted_sec / total_weighted_sec * 100`

Result is sorted by `focus_minutes` descending and returned as `top_presets`.

#### 4. Frontend: Rendering ([`dashboard.js:338–408`](file:///Users/harsh24/Desktop/Projects/Muhurata-focus-timer/static/js/dashboard.js#L338-L408))

`renderRhythms(data)`:
- Reads `data.top_presets`
- Renders two sort buttons (**Session** / **Share**, togglable with ↑↓)
- For each rhythm row, calculates the **bar width** as: `(focus_pct / maxShare) * 100%` — so the top entry always fills 100% and others are relative to it
- Displays `focus_minutes` formatted as `Xh Ym`

## 1. What counts as a "session" (data point)?

The current code already has the **bout** concept — consecutive productive segments grouped within a 2-minute gap, with a 5-minute noise floor. Each bout represents one continuous focus sitting. That's the natural unit.

So each data point on the bell curve = **one bout's total duration in minutes**.

Example from your data: if someone did 200 bouts over the year, we'd have 200 data points like `[14, 24, 36, 38, 24, 52, 12, 96, ...]`.

---

## 2. The honest truth: real session data won't be Gaussian

A real user's focus durations are shaped by the preset structure — you'd likely see **spikes at 12, 24, 36, 48 min** (the preset work durations) rather than a smooth bell. The distribution is also probably **right-skewed** (lots of normal sessions, a few unusually long ones).

This gives us two approaches:

### Option A — Fitted Gaussian (pretty but approximate)
Compute **mean** and **standard deviation** from bout durations, draw a smooth SVG bell curve. Simple, elegant, looks great. But it *lies* a little — it projects symmetry that may not exist.

### Option B — KDE (Kernel Density Estimation) (honest + smooth)
Place a small Gaussian "kernel" at each actual data point, sum them up. The result is a smooth curve that follows the **real shape** of your data — if it's skewed right, the curve shows that. If there are two peaks (e.g. you alternate between 24-min and 48-min sessions), it shows a bimodal hump.

> **My recommendation: Option B (KDE).** It gives you the bell-curve aesthetic when data is roughly normal, but honestly reveals the real shape. And the key insight you want — "where's my average, where's my max, where's my worst" — works with both approaches.

## Education: What each part of the bell curve tells you about yourself

Let me draw this out:

```
                          ← narrower = you're consistent →
                          ← wider = you're unpredictable →
     ▲
     │              ╭──╮
     │            ╭─╯  ╰─╮
     │          ╭─╯      ╰──╮
     │       ╭──╯            ╰───╮
     │   ╭───╯                   ╰─────╮
     │───╯                              ╰──────
     └───┬─────────┬─────────────┬──────────────→ minutes
       "off days"  PEAK         "flow days"
     (left tail)  (comfort)    (right tail)
```

### Peak (mode) vs Median — how they differ

| | Peak (mode) | Median |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The duration that appears **most often** — the tallest point of the curve | The **middle value** — 50% of sessions are shorter, 50% are longer |
| **What it tells you** | "My body/mind naturally settles here. This is my comfort zone." | "My *typical* session, accounting for both good and bad days." |
| **When they differ** | If your curve is skewed (long right tail from occasional deep-focus sessions), **the median gets pulled right** of the peak. Your peak might be 24 min but your median might be 28 min, because those occasional long sessions pull the middle up. |

In a perfectly symmetric bell, peak = median = mean. In real life they separate.

### What each region tells you

| Region | What it says | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| **Left tail** (worst ~10%) | "On my worst days, I can barely sit for X minutes" | Self-awareness — recognizing off days is fine, don't beat yourself up |
| **Peak** | "This is where I naturally land. My comfort zone." | **Goal setting** — this is the lever you want to push |
| **Right tail** (best ~10%) | "On my best days, I can sustain X minutes of deep focus" | Knowing your ceiling — what's *possible* for you |
| **Width (spread)** | Narrow = consistent performer. Wide = highly variable. | Tracking *consistency* improvement over time |

### What should you chase?

Your intuition is **exactly right**:

> "Look at the peak and set a goal for peak + 5 minutes so I can grow gradually"

**Yes. Chase the peak.** Here's why:

- The **right tail** is your rare flow state — it happens when conditions align perfectly. Chasing it daily leads to frustration.
- The **mean** gets inflated by those rare long sessions. Chasing it means chasing a number that doesn't reflect your typical capacity.
- The **peak** is your *actual, repeatable* ability. Moving it rightward by 5 minutes means your **whole distribution shifts right** — your typical day improves, your best days improve, even your worst days improve.

Over weeks/months, you'd literally watch the curve slide rightward. That's real growth.

## Decision: KDE (not fitted Gaussian)

Since your data is truly continuous and varied, **KDE** is the right choice. Here's the simple version of why:

- **Fitted Gaussian** says: "I assume your data is a perfect bell shape, let me draw one." If your data happens to have two humps (say you often do 20-min sessions AND 45-min sessions), the Gaussian would blur them into one meaningless blob in the middle.

- **KDE** says: "Let me look at your actual data and draw a smooth curve that follows its real shape." If there are two humps, you see two humps. If it's a bell, you see a bell.

KDE is just the honest version.
